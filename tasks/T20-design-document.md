**Depends on:** T17 results for the numbers · **Read:** all ADRs, all contracts, load-test report

## Goal
A **short** document (≤ ~8 pages) that defends the design in the author's absence. English only. Every number is either measured (say where) or labelled "estimate" with the formula. The challenge requires these sections; the notes below are the content to cover — write, verify, and replace estimates with measurements.

## Sections and required content
1. **Problem, assumptions, non-goals.** Assumptions to state: ≤ 10 k devices; 7 sensors at 1 Hz; one tenant; no auth (network-trusted); UTC; sensors are percentages; devices are Linux servers mostly; 1-year retention at hour resolution is enough, 1 s detail for 7 days; clock skew ignored (device timestamps trusted); chart points ≤ ~1 500. Deliberately not built: auth, multi-tenancy, alert engine, anomaly detection, cold-storage tiering, device auto-update, Windows fan support, push ingestion.
2. **Architecture** (diagram + one paragraph per component + failure table). Link ADRs.
3. **Storage choice** (ClickHouse) and why — short, links ADR-0002.
4. **How the 1-year chart is fast.** Tiering, planner, rows read (8 760/series), primary-key order matches the query, partition pruning, measured p50/p95 from the smoke/load test.
5. **Data volume and one-year cost.** Formulas (keep them in the doc):
   - rows/s = devices × 7 → 10 k devices = 70 000 rows/s = 6.05 B rows/day.
   - raw: rows/day × bytes/row; **estimate 2–4 B/row compressed** (DoubleDelta+Gorilla+ZSTD on sorted data) ⇒ ~12–24 GB/day at 10 k devices, ~85–170 GB for 7 days. 
   - `1m`: 7 × 1 440 = 10 080 rows/device/day; 90 days at 10 k devices ≈ 9.1 B rows; **estimate ~8 B/row** ⇒ ~70 GB.
   - `1h`: 7 × 8 760 = 61 320 rows/device/year; 10 k devices ≈ 613 M rows ⇒ ~5 GB.
   - Total at 10 k devices ≈ 160–270 GB (estimate) — dominated by raw and the 1m tier; cutting raw to 3 d and 1m to 30 d would save ~half. **Replace with measured `system.parts` bytes per row from the load test** and state the measured values.
   - Cost: `GB × price per GB-month` (state the price assumption, e.g. block storage) + replication factor + backup copies; note compute (writers, ClickHouse CPU/RAM) is usually larger than storage.
6. **Load test summary:** hardware, max sustained devices, the bottleneck and **why** (link graphs), and the 10 k-device answer: what breaks first and what changes. Start from these *hypotheses to confirm or refute with data* (don't copy them as facts): (a) single ClickHouse node CPU on insert+MV aggregation and background merges; (b) writer single-instance throughput / insert latency; (c) NATS fsync/disk throughput at 1 000 msg/s of ~15 KB; (d) scraper CPU for JSON decoding of 1 000 req/s; (e) the shared test host (simulator + everything on one machine). Remedies: shard scrapers by consistent hash of target URL (`--shard i/N`), scale writers on the shared durable consumer, `async_insert` or bigger batches, NATS 3-node cluster with `replicas=3`, ClickHouse 2 shards × 2 replicas with `Distributed` tables + Keeper, result cache for 1y queries.
7. **Growth plan — team of 4, 3 months, to 10 k devices.** Suggested split: *A: ingestion* (scraper sharding, discovery, backpressure, device lifecycle); *B: storage* (ClickHouse replicated/sharded, backups, retention, schema evolution); *C: bus & reliability* (NATS cluster, writer scale-out, DLQ handling, chaos tests); *D: product & ops* (query caching, auth/RBAC minimal, alerting, dashboards for the system itself, on-call docs). Timeline: wk 1–2 baseline + 1 k devices; wk 3–6 shard/replicate, 3 k → 6 k; wk 7–9 10 k with failure drills; wk 10–12 hardening, runbooks, capacity model, handover. Each week ends with a load-test run in CI-like environment. **Not building:** multi-tenancy, custom alert-rule UI, anomaly detection, per-metric retention, object-store tiering, Kubernetes operators.
8. **Migration plan — change the storage engine after a year, zero downtime, zero data loss.** Steps: (1) the API is the only reader and `store` is behind two small interfaces (`Inserter`, `Reader`) — ADR-0013; (2) provision new store + schema; (3) **dual-write**: start a second writer on a *new durable consumer* of the same stream (retention raised to cover the backfill duration); (4) **backfill history** from old store in time slices (rollups exported/imported, idempotent per slice; raw only inside retention); (5) **shadow reads**: query service executes each query against both stores, compares within tolerance, logs diffs, serves old; (6) parity checks (row counts and checksums per device/day); (7) cutover by percentage flag 1 % → 10 % → 100 %; (8) keep old store read-only for a rollback window, then decommission. Risks: stream retention shorter than backfill; semantic differences in rollups; double load during dual-write.
9. **Operations.** SLOs: query API availability 99.9 %/month; 1y chart p95 < 1 s, 5m chart p95 < 300 ms (server side); freshness p99 sample→visible < 30 s; completeness ≥ 99.9 % of expected samples from healthy devices; no loss of messages once published to JetStream. **Page at night** (user-visible or data-loss risk, needs action within the hour): writer not consuming (`consumer_pending` rising > 10 min or `e2e_lag_ms` p95 > 5 min); ClickHouse down/read-only; NATS down or stream storage > 85 % / filling within 4 h; ClickHouse disk full within 6 h; query availability fast burn (2 % of monthly budget in 1 h); fleet-wide completeness < 99 % for 15 min. **Ticket, business hours:** one or a few devices down; scrape failure rate 1–5 %; any `truncated_scrapes`; DLQ > 0; query p95 over SLO 30 min; disk > 70 %; parts-count/merge backlog trending up; certificate/image updates. Principle: page on symptoms, not causes; every page has a runbook link. Note what is not implemented in v1 (rules documented, not wired).
10. **Limitations and honest gaps.** Untested on Windows; disk_busy approximate off-Linux; fan only on Linux with hwmon; single-node compose; rollup min/max in backfill approximated; `devices` search is a scan (fine to ~100 k devices).
11. **What I'd do with more time** (short list).
